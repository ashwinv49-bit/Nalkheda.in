यह क्रम ही क्यों
Why This Order, Not the Reverse

The reverse direction is the same mileage, so people assume it does not matter. It does — but because of what has to be booked, not how far apart the temples are.

  • Nalkheda's hawan is arranged in person at the temple counter and cannot be pinned to an hour. It needs slack around it.
  • Ujjain's Bhasma Aarti is booked roughly 15 days ahead at mahakaleshwar.com, at 4:00 AM, and does not move.

Put the flexible stop first and the fixed one second, and a delay at Nalkheda costs you nothing. Do it the other way and a slow morning at Nalkheda puts your Bhasma Aarti slot at risk.

The expensive mistake

Starting at Omkareshwar looks tidy if you are flying into Indore. It is not — you end up crossing the same 156 km of the Indore–Nalkheda road twice, which adds roughly ₹6,000–7,000 of cab fare and most of a day, for no darshan you would not otherwise have had.

तीन दिन की योजना
Three Days, Done Properly

1️⃣
दिन 1 — नलखेड़ा पहुँचें
Day 1 · Arrive Nalkheda, stay the night

Evening aarti at 7:30 PM. Walk to the hawan counter the same evening and confirm tomorrow's slot — the counter is open until 11:00 PM, and doing this tonight rather than in the morning is the difference between a calm day and a lost one.

2️⃣
दिन 2 — हवन, फिर उज्जैन
Day 2 · Hawan in the morning, Ujjain by evening

6:00 AM aarti, then the hawan — allow three to four hours and plan nothing behind it. Leave for Ujjain by 2 PM, then Sandhya Aarti at Mahakaleshwar at 7:00 PM.

3️⃣
दिन 3 — भस्म आरती, फिर ओंकारेश्वर
Day 3 · Bhasma Aarti, then Omkareshwar

At the gate by 2:30–3:00 AM for the 4:00 AM Bhasma Aarti. Rest, then drive to Omkareshwar — 130 km, 2.5 hours. Keep the evening for the Narmada ghat; it is the quietest moment on the circuit.

Two days instead?

Workable for darshan only. You lose the 6:00 AM aarti at Nalkheda and any possibility of a hawan. If the hawan is your reason for travelling, take the third day — the extra night costs ₹1,000–1,500 and is the cheapest part of the trip.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the MP Shakti-Jyotirlinga circuit?
+

Three temples within a day's driving of each other in Madhya Pradesh: Maa Baglamukhi at Nalkheda, a Baglamukhi Siddha Peetha; Mahakaleshwar at Ujjain, a Jyotirlinga; and Omkareshwar, a second Jyotirlinga on an island in the Narmada. About 235 km of driving in total, comfortably done across three days.

In what order should I travel the circuit?
+

Nalkheda, then Ujjain, then Omkareshwar. Not because of distance — the reverse is the same mileage — but because of what has to be booked. Nalkheda's hawan is arranged in person and cannot be fixed to the hour, while Ujjain's Bhasma Aarti is booked about 15 days ahead and is immovable. Put the flexible stop first.

How many days does the circuit need?
+

Three days is comfortable and allows a hawan at Nalkheda. Two days covers darshan at all three but leaves no room for a hawan, and you will miss the 6 AM aarti at Nalkheda.

How far is Nalkheda from Ujjain and Omkareshwar?
+

Nalkheda to Ujjain is 100 km, about 2 hours by road. Ujjain to Omkareshwar is 130 km, about 2.5 hours via Indore. Nalkheda directly to Omkareshwar, skipping Ujjain, is 235 km and 5 to 5.5 hours.

Where should I start the circuit from?
+

Ujjain Junction if you are coming by train — it is far better connected than anywhere else on the route, and you can take Mahakaleshwar darshan the evening you arrive. Indore (IDR) is the nearest airport, 156 km from Nalkheda and 77 km from Omkareshwar.

What does the circuit cost?
+

Travelling as four and sharing cabs and rooms, roughly ₹5,000 to ₹8,400 per person for three days, excluding the hawan fee, which is set by the Nalkheda temple trust. Cabs are the largest line and split evenly, so a couple pays noticeably more per head than a group of four.
