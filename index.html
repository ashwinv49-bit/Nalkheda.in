The Siddha Peetha at Nalkheda

Maa Baglamukhi Mandir stands in Nalkheda town, Agar Malwa district, on the bank of the Lakhundar Nadi — known in older texts as the Lakshmana River, a tributary of the Narmada. It is one of only three principal Baglamukhi shrines in India; the others are at Datia in Madhya Pradesh and Kangra in Himachal Pradesh.

What makes Nalkheda distinct is the idol itself. Rather than a single murti, the sanctum holds a triad — Maa Baglamukhi at the centre, flanked by Maa Lakshmi and Maa Saraswati — representing the complete trinity of Shakti. The idol is described as self-manifested, and temple tradition places its establishment in the Dwapara Yuga, by the Pandavas under Lord Krishna's guidance before the Mahabharata war.

Who is Maa Baglamukhi

Baglamukhi is the eighth of the Ten Mahavidyas, the goddess of Stambhana Shakti — the power to still, restrain or paralyse opposing forces. She is worshipped as Pitambara Devi, the goddess clothed in yellow, and yellow runs through every part of her worship: the cloth, the flowers, the offerings.

The temple sits on a cremation ground, a shmashaan, which in the tantric tradition marks it as a potent site for sadhana. Sadhaks and saints travel here from across India for that reason, and the temple has a particular reputation among those facing litigation, disputes or obstruction — the domains Stambhana is invoked for.

Why most pilgrims come: the hawan

Nalkheda is above all a hawan temple. It has 75 hawan kunds, and the principal Manokamna Hawan runs about 45 minutes, performed by ordained pandits with the prescribed samagri — ghee, sarson, samidha and the rest.

Stambhan Puja is the other reason people travel here, performed under the guidance of experienced acharyas. Devotees come from across India and from abroad specifically for it.

How hawan booking actually works. At the temple counter in person, or online at the official government portal mabaglamukhi.com. Kunds are limited and fill up during Navratri and festival dates, so book at least a week ahead and carry photo ID.

Before you go

Wear yellow

Maa Baglamukhi is Pitambara Devi and yellow is her colour. Wearing yellow is considered auspicious and respectful. A yellow cloth or dupatta is the minimum.

Carry cash

Nalkheda is a small town. Draw cash in Ujjain or Indore before you travel — the few ATMs in the main market can run dry during festivals.

Book the hawan early

75 kunds fill quickly in Navratri and on festival dates. A week ahead is the minimum, and bring the ID you booked with.

Beware unofficial booking sites

Several sites claim to sell puja and darshan bookings for this temple, and fraud has been reported. Use mabaglamukhi.com or the temple office in person. Nobody legitimate will ask you to pay them for a hawan slot.

When the temple is busiest

Navratri is the peak by a distance — darshan runs later, processions fill the town on Ashtami and Navami, and rooms across Nalkheda, Agar Malwa and Ujjain sell out. Baglamukhi Jayanti, on Vaishakh Shukla Ashtami, is the temple's own festival and draws large crowds for special hawan and aarti. Chaitra Navratri in spring is quieter than Sharad Navratri and is worth considering if you would rather avoid the crush.
